## 0. The goal in one paragraph
A player makes their own character (name, gender, body build, face, hair, hair colour, skin tone) and equips a small gear set: head
armour, body armour, shield, and weapons sword / axe / spear / bow. Every combination animates consistently (idle, walk, attack, and the
same mounted on a horse) and other players see it. The character is a RIG: separate part sprites (head, torso, pelvis, upper arm,
forearm + hand, thigh, shin, foot, hair, helm, weapon, shield) on one shared skeleton, so gear and looks never need new frames.

**Done means:** the creator screen exists and saves; the in-game hero (on foot, mounted, in interiors, in other players' views) is drawn by the
rig with real part art; the server enforces the gear rules; tests pass; `MANUAL-TESTS.md` has a short checklist for James; `SPRITES.md`,
`ROADMAP.md` and `QUEUE.md` are updated.

## 1. Decisions James already made (do not reopen)
1. **A rig of separate parts.** James rejected rigs three times when the art was bad ("looks shit", "awful") and then asked for the rig again
   once it was built from real parts. Do not propose a different approach; make the rig look good with real parts.
2. **Body size varies only a small range:** about +-8% height (width varies more). NPCs, soldiers and every other human keep ONE size
   (`HUMAN_CHAR_PX = 25`, `client/src/worldVisuals.ts`); only the player's build changes it.
3. **A bow cannot be equipped together with a shield.** The server REFUSES the equip (either order: bow while a shield is on, or a shield while a
   bow is wielded). It is not "hide the shield".
4. **Gear is limited to** head armour, body armour, shield, and weapons sword / axe / spear / bow. (Legs, trinkets, club, staff stay in the code
   for relics and recruited heroes but the player character's sheet offers only these four slots.) Armour and weapons keep tiers 1-3.
5. **The game's art is PAINTED, not pixel art** (about 3,400 colours per 1,000 px, soft anti-aliased edges, ~2 px near-black rim `#191414`,
   light from the upper left; a person is ~98 px tall inside a 128 px frame and drawn at 25 px). Never draw a recognisable shape from scratch
   in code (it looks like a blob); harvest and recolour real art, or use art generated by James in ChatGPT. See `tools/sprite-making/SKILL.md`.
6. No old-save compatibility work: James starts new games.

## 4. The part art pipeline (how real art gets in)
James generates four sheets in ChatGPT from the prompts in `SPRITES.md` #106-#109 (limbs, torso + pelvis, heads / hair / helms, weapons + shield).
Each cell is 128 x 128 with ONE part drawn straight, side view facing right, at an exact anchor:

| family | anchor in the cell |
|---|---|
| limbs (arms, thigh, shin) | joint centre on the centre line, 20 px from the top |
| foot | ankle at x 44, y 36 (heel left, toe right) |
| torso | waist (bottom centre) at x 64, y 100 |
| pelvis | root at x 64, y 44 |
| head, hair, hair back, helm | neck base at x 64, y 96 (all on the SAME point so they overlay) |
| weapons | grip at x 64, y 20 (spear y 44; bow and shield centre y 64) |

Wire a sheet: `node scripts/slice-rig-sheet.mjs <sheet.png> scripts/rig-layouts/sheet-106-limbs.json --out client/public/rig/parts/chatgpt --merge`
(then `-107-torso`, `-108-heads`, `-109-weapons`). It crops, removes an opaque background, drops specks, rescales each part to the rig's sizes
(`expectH` in the layout), reads the pivot from the anchor (limbs also cross-checked against the joint cap, warns beyond 6 px), writes
`manifest.json`, `slice-report.json` (anything `CHECK` needs a look) and `slice-preview.png` (every part on its pivot). Then open
`rig/body.html?parts=chatgpt`. The first variant of each part is also saved under its bare name (`upperArm.cloth` -> `upperArm`), which is what the
rig loads today. `node scripts/test-slice-rig-sheet.mjs` tests the slicer (round trip and a messy sheet).

If a real sheet does not match the layout (different grid, parts off their anchors, shapes that do not suit the skeleton), fix the layout JSON or
tell James what to regenerate; do not redraw the art. If a part is missing, the placeholder from `parts/bodyset` stays as the fallback.

Placeholder proportions the rig assumes (source px; the real art is rescaled to them): thigh 14 + shin 10 + 11 below the ankle (`soleBelowAnkle`),
torso waist -> neck 31 and waist -> shoulder 26, upper arm 13, forearm 13 (+3 hand), head part ~29 above the neck base. Bone lengths must add up to
the hip height, or the idle stance crouches (a lesson from the prototypes).

## 6. Quality bar and how to verify your own work
- Always compare new art / animation SIDE BY SIDE with the game's real hero at in-game size (the sheet tools draw both). If it only looks good
  enlarged, it is wrong. Judge frozen frames from the contact sheet, not only motion.
- Type-check `npx tsc --noEmit -p server` and `-p client` (and shared). Targeted tests only.
- Client build for a visual check goes to a scratch folder: `cd client && npx vite build --outDir <scratch>/dist --emptyOutDir`; serve it on a private port
  and point it at a test server on a private port (the client hard-codes `ws://localhost:8080`; the earlier checks rewrote that URL in a Playwright init script,
  see `tools/rigging/sheet.mjs` and the way `rig` pages are tested).
- Report to James what you checked and what you did not.

## 7. Pitfalls seen so far
- Authoring at the on-screen size (25 px) or drawing forms from nothing both failed. Work at source scale (98 px person) and use real art.
- Rotating whole-character art (cut-outs of `heroes/base-0x`) gives lumpy limbs and knee-less legs; that is why the parts must be drawn for the rig.
- The hero art is a 3/4 view; the rig parts are pure side view. Torso, head and feet must be side-on too or the mix looks wrong.
- The foot must be its own part (flat sole while planted); no knee / elbow "balls" (continue trouser / sleeve across the joint).
- `rig/body.html` fetches `manifest.json`, so it needs HTTP, not `file://`.
- `main.ts` is huge; add the compositor in a new module and touch `main.ts` minimally.
- Interiors scale the hero by measured body height (`humanScale`); a rig frame must report a sensible measured height.
- Do not break the current hero while parts are missing: fall back to `heroArt`.
